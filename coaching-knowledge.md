# Coached by Dejan — Coaching Knowledge Base

## Brand Philosophy
- Sustainable transformation over quick fixes
- Never extreme, never punishing — always supportive
- Training should feel enjoyable, especially in the beginning
- The goal is to build a lifestyle, not survive a programme
- Feminine, warm communication — coach as a supportive partner
- Results come from consistency, not intensity

## Primary Client Profile
- Women aged 30-70
- Primary goal: weight loss and body toning
- Most are beginners or returning after a long break
- Often low confidence around exercise
- Need to feel safe, supported, and successful early on

---

## Programme Structure — Beginner (12 Weeks)

### Weekly Progression
- **Weeks 1-3:** 3 training days per week
- **Weeks 4-12:** 4 training days per week (reviewed on monthly call)
- Rest days are as important as training days — always include them
- Active recovery (walking, stretching) encouraged on rest days

### Weekly Split — 3 Days (Weeks 1-3)
- Day 1: Lower Body & Glutes
- Day 2: Upper Body & Core
- Day 3: Full Body (lighter, more movement-based)
- Example: Monday / Wednesday / Friday

### Weekly Split — 4 Days (Weeks 4-12)
- Day 1: Lower Body & Glutes (heavy focus)
- Day 2: Upper Body Push (chest, shoulders, triceps)
- Day 3: Lower Body & Glutes (lighter, more reps)
- Day 4: Upper Body Pull & Core (back, biceps, abs)
- Example: Monday / Tuesday / Thursday / Friday

### Session Length
- Weeks 1-3: 30-40 minutes
- Weeks 4-8: 45-50 minutes
- Weeks 9-12: 50-60 minutes

## Training Principles

### Week 1 Philosophy
- Light loads, simple movements
- Focus on learning the exercises, not the weight
- Keep it fun and achievable — client must leave feeling good, not destroyed
- No HIIT, no circuits, no supersets in week 1
- Straight sets with adequate rest (60-90 seconds)
- End every session with a sense of accomplishment

### Progressive Overload (Week by Week)
- Weeks 1-2: Learn movements, establish baseline weights
- Weeks 3-4: Increase reps or add 1 set
- Weeks 5-6: Increase weight slightly (5-10% on machines)
- Weeks 7-8: Introduce supersets on isolation exercises
- Weeks 9-10: Introduce drop sets on key exercises
- Weeks 11-12: Peak intensity — client is now trained enough to push

### Cardio Progression
- Weeks 1-3: No dedicated HIIT. Light cardio warmup only (5-10 min walk/bike)
- Weeks 4-6: Introduce steady state cardio (20 min moderate pace) after strength
- Weeks 7-9: Introduce HIIT intervals (20 sec on / 40 sec off x 8 rounds) once per week
- Weeks 10-12: 2x HIIT sessions per week, increasing intensity

## Exercise Selection

### Priority Areas (Women — Weight Loss & Toning)
1. Glutes and posterior chain — always the priority
2. Legs (quads, hamstrings)
3. Core (functional, not just crunches)
4. Upper body for posture and tone (not bulk)

### Preferred Exercises — Lower Body & Glutes
**Machines first:**
- Leg Press (replaces barbell squat for beginners)
- Hip Abductor Machine
- Hip Adductor Machine
- Leg Curl (hamstrings)
- Leg Extension (quads)
- Glute Kickback Machine
- Smith Machine Hip Thrust (or free weight with bench)

**Free weight alternatives:**
- Dumbbell Romanian Deadlift
- Dumbbell Sumo Squat
- Dumbbell Hip Thrust
- Resistance Band Glute Kickbacks
- Bodyweight Glute Bridges

### Preferred Exercises — Upper Body
**Machines first:**
- Chest Press Machine
- Seated Row Machine
- Lat Pulldown Machine
- Shoulder Press Machine
- Cable Bicep Curl
- Cable Tricep Pushdown

**Free weight alternatives:**
- Dumbbell Bench Press
- Dumbbell Row
- Dumbbell Shoulder Press
- Dumbbell Bicep Curl
- Dumbbell Tricep Extension

### Core
- Plank (start 20 seconds, build to 60)
- Dead Bug
- Bird Dog
- Seated Ab Machine
- Cable Crunch
- Leg Raises (from bench or hanging)

### Exercises to Avoid for Beginners
- Heavy barbell back squat (too technical, injury risk)
- Heavy deadlift (save for intermediate)
- Olympic lifts
- Box jumps or high-impact plyometrics (weeks 1-6)
- Behind-the-neck press

## Sets, Reps & Rest — Guidelines

### Weeks 1-3 (Foundation)
- Sets: 2-3
- Reps: 12-15 (light weight, focus on form)
- Rest: 60-90 seconds between sets
- RPE (effort): 5-6 out of 10

### Weeks 4-6 (Building)
- Sets: 3
- Reps: 10-12
- Rest: 60 seconds
- RPE: 6-7 out of 10

### Weeks 7-9 (Developing)
- Sets: 3-4
- Reps: 8-12
- Rest: 45-60 seconds
- Introduce supersets on isolation exercises
- RPE: 7-8 out of 10

### Weeks 10-12 (Peak)
- Sets: 3-4
- Reps: 8-12 with drop sets on final set of key exercises
- Rest: 45 seconds
- RPE: 8-9 out of 10

## Nutrition Framework

### Goal: Sustainable Fat Loss
- Caloric deficit of 300-500 kcal per day — never more
- Never below 1200 kcal/day for women
- Adequate protein is the most important macro for body composition

### Macro Targets
- **Protein:** 1.6-2.0g per kg of bodyweight (priority)
- **Carbohydrates:** 40-45% of total calories (energy for training)
- **Fat:** 25-30% of total calories (hormones, satiety)

### Calorie Calculation Method
1. Calculate TDEE (Total Daily Energy Expenditure):
   - Sedentary (desk job, no exercise): BMR × 1.2
   - Lightly active (1-3 days training): BMR × 1.375
   - Moderately active (3-5 days): BMR × 1.55
2. Subtract 300-500 kcal for fat loss target
3. Set protein first, fill remaining calories with carbs and fat

### BMR Calculation (Mifflin-St Jeor for women)
BMR = (10 × weight in kg) + (6.25 × height in cm) - (5 × age) - 161

### Meal Timing Guidance
- Eat protein with every meal
- Pre-workout: light carbs + protein 1-2 hours before
- Post-workout: protein within 1-2 hours after
- Avoid skipping meals — leads to overeating later
- If client eats late: focus on protein and vegetables in the evening

### Hydration
- Minimum 2L water per day
- More on training days

## Special Cases

### Injuries & Limitations
- Always read PAR-Q answers from intake form
- Knee problems: avoid leg extension, replace with leg press at partial range, wall sits
- Lower back: avoid heavy deadlifts, replace with cable pull-throughs or glute bridges
- Shoulder problems: avoid overhead press, replace with lateral raises and cable rows
- Pregnancy/postpartum: avoid direct ab work, heavy loading, high impact
- General rule: work around limitations, never through pain

### Older Clients (50+)
- Longer warmup (10 minutes)
- More rest between sets (90 seconds)
- Focus on mobility alongside strength
- Lower impact cardio (walking, cycling, swimming)
- Higher rep ranges (15-20) with lighter weight to reduce joint stress
- Add balance exercises

### Very Deconditioned Clients
- Start with 2 days per week if 3 feels too much
- Bodyweight or resistance band only for first 2 weeks
- Focus on movement patterns before adding load
- Celebrate every small win — confidence is the first goal

## Programme Tone & Communication
- Sessions should have names that feel aspirational, not clinical
- Descriptions should be warm and encouraging
- Never use language like "burn fat" or "destroy" — use "build", "strengthen", "progress"
- Each session note from coach should feel personal and supportive
- The client should always know why she is doing what she is doing
